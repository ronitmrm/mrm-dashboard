Section 01 · Foundation

Purpose

Section 01Summary

Why MRM exists, where it is going, and what it makes. Everything else in this guide is downstream of these three statements.

01.1Mission

Why we exist

We exist to make precision manufacturing easy to buy. Our customers get full traceability, fast answers, and one team that owns their project from enquiry to delivery. In an industry that too often goes quiet, we stay reachable, and we solve the problem in front of us.

The mission is a promise about friction, not about quality. Competitors can match a tolerance. Far fewer answer the email, trace the material, and give the client a name to call. That is the gap MRM fills, and the proof of the mission is operational: response time, traceability, and a single point of contact, not the word "easy" on its own.

01.2Vision

MRM's vision holds two ambitions as one discipline. The rigour that earns world-class clients is the same rigour that lets MRM look after its people. Excellence pays for care. Care produces excellence. They are not a trade-off. They are the same job, run in both directions.

External vision

Client-facing

To become the manufacturing partner that world-class clients rely on, and to show that operational excellence and the wellbeing of our workforce are not a trade-off but the same commitment. The rigour our customers see in every part is the rigour we bring to every life inside MRM.

Internal vision

Internal only · not for client use

Be undeniable. Run the floor so tightly that world-class clients cannot ignore us, grow the margins that make it real, and put the winnings where they belong: into the health, pay, and futures of the people who make MRM. Being this good and this generous is one job, not two. Excellence pays for care, and care produces excellence.

The internal vision names commercial intent plainly and is written for the team, from the CEO to the floor. Keep it in internal documents. Use the external vision in anything a client may read.

Proof, not aspiration

The "care" half of the vision is already in motion, which keeps it honest. Projects in the pipeline include full-time schooling for staff and workers' families, all-inclusive medical and healthcare camps, and on-campus recreational space. These are the evidence the vision points to, and they should be cited wherever the vision is stated.

01.3What we do

MRM manufactures precision brass and metal fittings for fluid and gas systems. The range spans a dozen product categories and more than 2,000 SKUs, across lead-free and leaded brass, steel, stainless, and copper, serving plumbing, HVAC, hydraulic, pneumatic, automotive, agriculture, electrical, and energy and gas applications.

Every part is made in-house, from raw material through machining, assembly, and quality control. Beyond the catalogue, MRM develops custom components with clients end to end: research, design, testing, and stocking, with a dedicated team as the single point of contact. This turnkey work is still fittings; it is the catalogue extended to a client's specific need. Certified for potable water and built for traceability, MRM supplies distributors and OEMs across the US, UK, and EU.

Lead-free and potable-water capability is the most defensible single differentiator, but it is one application among several. Water leads today; automotive, HVAC, energy, and the rest are equal parts of the range.

Section 02 · Foundation

Brand story

Section 02Narrative

The story is about who MRM is today, not where it came from. It is a positioning narrative, written in the brand voice, and it models the standard all MRM copy is held to.

MRM makes precision brass and metal fittings for systems that cannot afford to fail.

Manufacturing since 2008 in Jamnagar, MRM supplies OEMs and distributors across the US, UK, and EU with fittings for drinking water, gas, hydraulics, automotive, and industrial systems. More than a dozen product categories, over 2,000 SKUs, across lead-free and leaded brass, steel, stainless, and copper.

What sets MRM apart is not the catalogue, it is the discipline behind it. Everything is made in-house and traced end to end. Lead-free parts are NSF-certified for potable water, a capability still rare among Indian manufacturers. Custom components are developed with the client from research through stocking. And whatever the order, the customer deals with one dedicated team, not a switchboard.

Precise where it counts. Accountable at every step. Easy to work with throughout.

02.1How to use the story

This is the master narrative. Shorten it for a website hero, a line-card, or a capability deck, but do not add claims it does not make, and keep the closing three-beat intact wherever length allows. It is the tone reference for every piece of MRM copy: declarative, specific, and free of unproven praise.

Note on origin: the company anchors to "manufacturing since 2008". Earlier incorporation dates and ownership history are not part of the brand story and are not used in brand or marketing material.

Section 03 · Foundation

Audience

Section 03Who we serve

MRM sells business to business, never to end users. The customer is a company that buys fittings by the thousand and puts them into its own products or its own distribution.

03.1Primary · OEMs and manufacturers

Companies that build MRM's fittings into their own finished products: HVAC units, automotive and air-brake systems, hydraulic and pneumatic assemblies, agricultural machinery, energy and gas equipment. They are the core customer. MRM's part becomes part of their product, so its tolerance, its compliance, and its traceability become their problem too.

What the primary customer needs, in order

Compliance they can prove downstream: NSF for potable water, DOT and SAE for air-brake and fluid systems, ISO-certified manufacturing. Then traceability: every part back to its material and inspection record. Then custom capability: the ability to co-develop a part that does not exist yet, from research and design through testing and stocking. Then reliability: consistent tolerance at volume, and a supply that holds.

03.2Secondary · Distributors and procurement

Distributors in the US, UK, and EU who resell MRM's fittings under their own catalogues to plumbers, contractors, and trade counters. And the procurement teams, at both OEMs and distributors, who actually place the order. Distributors care about consistent supply, correct documentation, and private-label and retail packaging. Procurement cares about quote response time, landed cost, lead-time reliability, and one named contact instead of a call centre.

03.3Sectors served

Plumbing, HVAC, hydraulic, pneumatic, automotive, agriculture equipment, electrical, and energy and gas.

Who MRM is not for

Individual retail buyers, one-off orders, and unregulated commodity work where price is the only question. MRM's advantages, compliance and traceability and responsiveness, are worth nothing to a buyer who only wants the cheapest barb. The right customer is one for whom documentation and reliability are worth paying for.

Archetype & promise

Section 04Concept

Everything MRM says and makes traces back to one idea: authority built on craftsmanship. Two archetypes hold that idea in tension, and the tension is the point.

Archetype I

Ruler

Control. Order. Stability. Fittings govern the flow of fluid and gas. They hold under pressure. They sit inside systems where failure has consequences.

  • Assured, matter-of-fact statements
  • Stability the customer can plan around
Archetype II

Technical intelligence. Manufacturing pride. MRM engineers custom solutions and works daily in tolerances and precision machining. It solves problems, it does not just supply parts.

  • Engineering intelligence
  • Problem-solving over order-taking
  • Pride in how the work is made
The promise

Control, flexibility, and stability, all under pressure.

The promise is an internal north star. It is not external copy in this literal form, and the word "flexibility" always arrives with proof beside it (see Section 05).

04.1Personality

MRM differentiates itself from old-school corporates. It speaks like someone who solves problems, the way a technician explains something to a trusted partner, not the way a company advertises to a consumer.

The reference point the founders use: MRM is the cool teacher who finds joy in new ways to teach. It sounds wise, it is kind, it is welcoming. It is the opposite of all-jargon-zero-credibility. Talking to MRM should feel worth the person's time. MRM is secular and positive.

Section 05 · Verbal

Voice

Section 05Core tone

MRM makes components for systems where failure has consequences. The writing carries the same properties as the product: measured, unambiguous, built to hold under pressure.

Core tone

Calm authority with human consideration. State facts, do not sell them. Speak like a technician explaining something to a colleague they respect, not like a company advertising to a stranger.

The voice is

  • Controlled. "We machine to any tolerance you specify."
  • Declarative. "Rated for pressure systems up to 25 bar."
  • Precise. "Compression fittings in certified European brass grades."
  • Human. "Please share the drawing and we will confirm feasibility."

The voice is not

  • Boastful. "Best-in-class performance."
  • Vague. "High-quality solutions for all your needs."
  • Cold. "Send the details."
  • Corporate. "We would like to take this opportunity to inform you."

Section 06 · Verbal

The proof rule

Section 06The rule

This is the rule the whole vocabulary hangs from. It replaces a blanket ban on evaluative words, which failed because it could not tell a hollow claim from a caption on a wall of evidence.

The rule

An evaluative word is permitted only when its proof is in the same field of view. No proof visible, no adjective.

"Trusted partner" on a homepage is unsupported. Nothing on the screen earns it, so the reader is asked to take it on faith. That is exactly the move MRM does not make. But "Trusted components for critical systems" set above four certification marks is a different act: the certificates are the proof, and the phrase becomes a caption to evidence rather than a claim floating in air.

Some words can never clear the bar, because nothing can prove them. Premium. World-class. Leading. Cutting-edge. Delete on sight. Others are conditional. Trusted, reliable, proven, certified, precise become available the moment the evidence is adjacent, and unavailable the moment it is not.

WordStatusCondition
CertifiedConditionalName the certificate. "NSF-certified", not "certified quality".
PreciseConditionalGive the number. "±0.005 mm" beats "precise" every time.
ReliableConditionalShow the mechanism: traceability, inspection rate, retention period.
TrustedConditionalOnly with client marks or certifications in the same view.
FlexibleConditionalDefine it at once: multi-alloy capability, three-month inventory holds, pivoting to a changed spec mid-run.
PremiumNeverUnprovable. Remove.
World-classNeverUnprovable. Remove.
InnovativeNear-neverOnly with the specific innovation named. Otherwise remove.

Why this beats a word list: a ban teaches people to avoid words. This rule teaches them to look for evidence, which is the habit MRM actually wants. If the proof is not there, the problem was never the adjective. The problem is that there is nothing to say.

Section 07 · Verbal

Sentence rhythm

Section 07Cadence

Rhythm is what makes writing from four different people sound like one company. It is the most transferable part of this guide and the easiest to get wrong.

Rule 1 · One idea per sentence

Designed for water systems and industrial use and high-pressure applications.
Designed for potable water systems. Suitable for high-pressure applications.

Rule 2 · Short to medium, never long

MRM is a leading manufacturer that provides high-quality solutions across a wide variety of industries and applications.
MRM manufactures precision brass components for fluid systems.

Rule 3 · The three-beat pattern

The house cadence: statement, supporting detail, specification. It moves from what a thing is, to what it is for, to what can be verified, which is the order a technical reader wants.

Designed for potable water systems.
Manufactured in lead-free brass.
NSF-certified.

Rule 4 · Cut the runway

Filler openings delay the only sentence that matters. Delete them and start at the fact.

It is important to note that we would like to inform you that the shipment may be subject to a slight delay.
The shipment is delayed by 3 days.

08.1Consistency of terms

The same thing gets the same name every time. Switching vocabulary to avoid repetition reads as literary in an essay and as sloppy in a specification, and in a technical business it introduces real ambiguity about whether two words mean two things.

Always

  • compression fittings
  • components
  • tolerance
  • lead-free brass

Never drift into

  • fittings / connectors / units / items
  • solutions / products / offerings
  • accuracy / exactness / precision level
  • eco brass / green brass / safe brass
Section 09 · Verbal

The human layer

Section 09Register

Declarative is not the same as blunt. The two live in different places, and confusing them is the most likely way this guide gets misapplied.

The split

When addressing a person, be considerate. When describing a product, be declarative. A spec sheet never says "please". An email always does.

SurfaceRegisterExample
Email, chat, callsConsiderate"Please share the drawing so we can review the tolerance."
Quotes & proposalsConsiderate"Let us know if the lead time works and we will confirm the slot."

09.1Respect without softness

Consideration is not hedging. "Please share the remaining information" is respectful and direct. It names the gap and asks for the fix.

Data incomplete.
The data is incomplete. Please share the remaining information.
Sorry to bother you, but whenever you get a chance, would it be possible to maybe look at the drawing?
Please review the drawing and let us know by Thursday.

Section 11 · Verbal

Writing mechanics

Section 11Mechanics

Small rules that keep every document looking like it came from the same hand.

Punctuation · the em-dash rule

Write without em-dashes. As a rule, MRM copy does not use them. Where a break is needed, use a full stop, a comma, or a colon. The em-dash is reserved for rare, deliberate impact and appears very occasionally, if at all.

Do

  • Full stop for two independent clauses. "The data is incomplete. Please resend it."
  • Comma for a tight aside. "Machined in lead-free brass, tested to 25 bar."
  • Colon to introduce a list or an explanation.
  • En-dash for numeric ranges only. "45–75 characters", "2–3 metres".

Don't

  • No em-dash as a default connector.
  • No underlines, anywhere, including the company name.
  • No italics for emphasis. Use weight.
  • No exclamation marks in technical or client copy.

Five lockups. The choice is governed by two questions, in order: is this internal or external? Then, how much width is available?

Internal documents never carry the tagline. External documents always do, at the longest length the space allows.

Section 17 · Visual

The CAD motif

Section 17Motif

The recurring line-art of a fitting, drawn in the style of a CAD or engineering drawing, is a deliberate brand motif. It signals precisely what MRM does and how it thinks, and it is retained.

The motif works because it is honest. It shows the product as the people who make it see it: as geometry with dimensions and tolerances, not as a glamour shot. It carries the Creator archetype directly.

Use it as a light background element, a section divider, or a hero on technical pages. Keep it line-only, single-colour, and never let it compete with live content.

Do

  • Draw it single-weight in MRM Green, or ghosted at low opacity behind content.
  • Include dimension lines and callouts. The engineering language is the point.
  • Keep it accurate to a real fitting geometry.

Don't

  • No photoreal rendering mixed with the line motif.
  • No signal or crate colours on the linework.
  • Never place it so densely that it reduces legibility of text over it.

Structure carries meaning

Numbers appear only where there is a sequence. Rules appear only where there is a division. An arrow appears only where something moves. If a device is not encoding something true, remove it. Section 19 · Visual

Safety & operational documents

Section 19Register 1

SOPs, hazard notices, and shop-floor signage are read under stress, in bad light, by someone who may be frightened. They use a stricter subset of the system, and they follow a fixed anatomy so any two posters in the plant look like one family.

Art direction runs in registers

Different surfaces do different jobs, so the brand carries more than one art direction. A shop-floor safety poster is calm and sparse. A recruitment standee is bold and energetic. Both are correct, and both are unmistakably MRM, because they share one foundation: the palette, the logo, flat fills, Tennis restraint, and the proof rule. What changes between registers is mood and layout, never the core. This section is Register 1 · Safety & operational. The next section is Register 2 · Marketing & outreach. More registers will be added as MRM expands into internal notices, awareness, and public-facing work.

19.2The step card

Structure

Rounded card, light background, 1px border. Inside: a pictogram, then the Gujarati line, then the Hindi line. A "do not" step turns its card and icon Danger-red and uses a prohibition pictogram. A normal step uses Green.

Bilingual rule for signage

On life-safety signage, Gujarati and Hindi carry equal visual weight. This overrides the general "local language leads" rule used elsewhere, because in an emergency a reader must find their language instantly, not hunt for a secondary line. English is optional here and, if present, is a small reference label only.

Iconography exception

Safety signage uses standard ISO 3864 / ISO 7010 pictograms, not the brand line-icon set. A recognised prohibition circle or exit runner is read faster under panic than an elegant custom stroke. Recolour pictograms to the MRM signal palette where possible, but never redraw them into something unfamiliar. Recognition beats style here.

19.5Art direction

The rules above make a poster correct. These make it look like MRM. They are measured from the reference builds, not invented, and they are what separate a calm, authoritative poster from a cluttered one.

The one rule that matters most

Roughly two-thirds of a safety poster is empty background. Space is the design. If a layout feels full, it is wrong, remove content or split it across two posters before you tighten the spacing.

Colour budget

Measured across the reference builds, the ink budget is strict. Green carries structure. Signal colours are used in single-digit percentages, never as decoration. Keep to these proportions and a poster stays calm; exceed them and it starts to shout.

Proportion & spacing

ElementDirection
FormatPortrait, near A-series (roughly 1:1.4). Print target is A-size for wall mounting.
Outer marginKeep a visible cream margin on all four sides. The green frame or content floats; it never bleeds to the edge. Aim for 4 to 6 percent of width.
Card paddingGenerous. Interior padding on a step card is larger than the gap between cards, so each card reads as a calm, self-contained unit rather than a crammed cell.
Title blockLarge and confident. The bilingual instruction is the loudest text on the page after the header, set in a solid green panel with real breathing room around the words.
One idea per cardA card holds one step and its pictogram. Never two instructions in a card, never a paragraph. If it needs a paragraph, it is not signage.

Tone of the layout

Aim for

Calm authority. Even spacing, few colours, large legible type, one clear reading path top to bottom. It should look like it was made by the same company that machines to five microns.

Avoid

Clip-art icons, more than three signal colours in view, drop shadows and bevels, full-bleed colour, text that fills its container edge to edge, and any element that does not carry information.

Reference builds: MRM-QA-015-75 (Fire Extinguisher Use) and MRM-QA-015-86 (Fire Safety Tips) are the canonical examples of this anatomy. New safety posters should match their structure.

Section 20 · Visual

Marketing & outreach

Section 20Register 2

Register 2. Front-facing marketing: standees, posters, flyers, social, backdrops, brochures, recruitment. Where safety signage is calm and sparse, marketing is allowed to be bold, figure-driven, and energetic. It stays MRM through the shared foundation, but it carries its own component set. This register is locked from the approved Campus Placement standee and poster.

20.1Two base modes

Every marketing layout uses one of two modes. Never mix them within a single artboard. Both carry the same components; only the ground flips.

Mode A · Green base

Full MRM Green field. Cream type and shapes float on it. For standees, backdrops, social, anything that must dominate at distance.

Mode B · Cream base

Cream field inside a Green border frame, with a rounded cream card holding the content. For posters, flyers, notices, anything read up close or on paper.

20.4Do not

Marketing don'ts

  • Mix Mode A and Mode B grounds in one artboard.
  • Use Tennis as a background, or add a third accent colour.
  • Fill, colour, or arbitrarily rotate the CAD motif.
  • Put text over the figures.
  • Use a solid rule where the dotted rule belongs.
  • Exceed two lines on a headline, or add gradients, shadows, or bevels.

Marketing do's

  • Pick one mode and commit the whole artboard to it.
  • Give Tennis exactly one job per zone.
  • Keep the descriptor line on anything a stranger sees.
  • Lead with the dotted rule and the short Tennis dash as signatures.
  • Keep figures in the human zone, CAD ghosted and technical.
  • Run CTA copy as short two-tone beats.

Reference builds: the Campus Placement standee (Mode A) and poster (Mode B). New marketing pieces match their construction. This register is locked; layouts flex within it, the rules do not.
